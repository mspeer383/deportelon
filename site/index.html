Summary

Elon Musk has been a U.S. citizen since 2002. Before that, according to company documents, court records, former business associates and his own statements, he worked in the United States without authorization while launching his first company.

Musk disputes this. He says he was in fact allowed to work in the US on a J-1 visa that transitioned to an H1-B.

This page sets out the documented record. Every statement links to its source. Quotes are verbatim. Where the record is disputed, reversed on appeal or unresolved, the page says so.

III. Legal record

Current outcomes as of October 2026. Settlements are listed as settlements: in each one, Musk did not admit wrongdoing. Cases that were reversed in his favor are listed as such.

Rulings against Musk that still stand

SEC "funding secured" (2018)
Musk and Tesla each paid a $20 million penalty to settle SEC charges over his 2018 tweets about taking Tesla private, without admitting or denying the allegations. Musk stepped down as Tesla chairman. His bid to undo the settlement failed: the Second Circuit ruled for the SEC, and the Supreme Court declined to hear the case on April 29, 2024. A separate investor class action over the same tweets ended in a 2023 jury verdict for Musk, which was upheld on appeal. SEC · UPI
Twitter investors' lawsuit, Pampena v. Musk (2026, on appeal)
On March 20, 2026, a federal jury found that two of Musk's May 2022 statements about Twitter were false or misleading, and found for Musk on a third statement and on the "scheme" claims. In July 2026 the judge set aside the verdict on one statement (the May 17 tweet) and left the rest in place. Musk has appealed to the Ninth Circuit. Damages are not final. Al Jazeera · Court order · Appeal docket

Settled without admitting wrongdoing

SEC Twitter-stake disclosure suit (2025–26)
The SEC sued in January 2025, alleging Musk disclosed his Twitter stake late and underpaid other shareholders by at least $150 million. It settled for a $1.5 million penalty paid by his trust, without admitting or denying. In approving it in July 2026, the judge noted significant misgivings. SEC complaint · SEC settlement · Quartz
Owen Diaz v. Tesla (race discrimination)
A 2021 jury awarded about $137 million. The judge cut it, and a 2023 retrial set damages at about $3.2 million. The case settled in 2024 on undisclosed terms. AP via KTVU

Reversed or dropped in Musk's favor

NLRB: 2018 union tweet
The NLRB ordered the tweet deleted and found that a union supporter had been unlawfully fired. In October 2024 the full Fifth Circuit vacated the order, ruling that the NLRB erred in ordering the deletion of Musk's speech, and sent the firing finding back to the Board. Fifth Circuit opinion
Tornetta v. Musk (Tesla pay package)
Delaware's Chancery Court rescinded Musk's 2018 pay package in January 2024. In December 2025 the Delaware Supreme Court reversed that remedy and restored the package. Reuters via Yahoo Finance
DOJ v. SpaceX (hiring of refugees and asylees)
DOJ alleged in 2023 that SpaceX discouraged refugees and asylees from applying for jobs. DOJ dropped the case with prejudice in February 2025. There was no finding against SpaceX. NBC News · Immigration Policy Tracking Project
Brody defamation case
In March 2026 a Texas appeals court held that Musk's post was protected opinion. FindLaw

Pending: allegations only

EEOC v. Tesla (racial harassment, Fremont factory)
Filed in 2023. The parties went to private mediation in 2026. No ruling. Tesla denies the allegations. EEOC · HR Dive
California Civil Rights Department v. Tesla
The race discrimination case is in trial before a judge from September 21, 2026, scheduled through October 30. No ruling yet. CRD · JURIST
$1 million election giveaway
Philadelphia's district attorney sued in 2024. The court denied an injunction, and the suit was later dropped. Class actions in Texas are proceeding, and Musk has been ordered to be deposed. There is no final ruling. Philadelphia Inquirer · Reuters via GV Wire

IV. The open question

Federal law allows the government to revoke citizenship that was illegally procured or obtained by concealment of a material fact or by willful misrepresentation (8 U.S.C. § 1451(a)).

In June 2025, the Justice Department told its Civil Division to prioritize and maximally pursue denaturalization proceedings in all cases permitted by law and supported by the evidence. One priority category it named is citizenship acquired through government corruption, fraud, or material misrepresentations. DOJ memo

Musk's immigration file is not public. What he disclosed about his 1995–97 work in later visa, green card and naturalization filings is not known, and USCIS will not discuss specific individuals.

We ask USCIS and the Department of Justice to review whether Elon Musk's unauthorized work in 1995–97, as documented above, was fully disclosed in his immigration and naturalization filings, under the same standard the government applies to everyone else.
